My Claude Usage Strategy
Personalized for: Student | Daily User | Learning + Career Prep | Fast Answers + Creative Work

Recommended Primary Model
🎯 Claude Sonnet 5

Why This Model Fits Me
Your profile is a sweet spot for Sonnet, not Opus or Haiku:

You're a student, not a researcher or enterprise team — you don't need Opus-level deep reasoning for most daily tasks.
Learning + Resume/Career Prep both need explanations that are accurate but also well-written — Sonnet balances intelligence with strong natural writing, which Haiku can't match.
Creative Work (LinkedIn posts, resume phrasing, study content) needs a model with good "voice" — Sonnet is tuned for this; Haiku tends to sound flatter and more mechanical.
Daily use means cost and speed matter — running everything on Opus would be overkill and slower than necessary for routine work.
Fast Answers are still well-served by Sonnet at lower effort — you don't need to switch models just to get quick responses; you adjust the effort dial instead (more on this below).


When to Use Haiku
⚡ Claude Haiku 4.5
Use for:

Quick factual lookups ("what's the syntax for X", "define Y term")
Simple formatting or cleanup tasks
High-volume, low-stakes questions where you just need a fast fact, not a polished answer
Rapid back-and-forth brainstorming where speed > depth


When to Use Sonnet
🧠 Claude Sonnet 5 (your default)
Use for:

Explaining concepts for study/learning
Drafting and polishing resume content, cover letters, LinkedIn posts
Creative writing (captions, hooks, post copy, journal entries)
Day-to-day Q&A that needs nuance, not just facts
Career prep tasks — mock interview answers, skill-gap analysis, career strategy


When to Use Opus
🚀 Claude Opus 4.8
Use for (occasionally, not daily):

A high-stakes deliverable — final resume before a real application, a scholarship essay, a major assignment
Complex multi-step reasoning — e.g., planning a full semester study strategy, analyzing a tricky academic problem
When Sonnet's answer feels shallow on something genuinely hard and you need the extra depth


Recommended Effort Level
Low
Quick fact checks, definitions, simple rewording — when you just need speed.
Standard (your everyday default — labeled "High" in the Claude interface)
Learning explanations, resume bullet writing, LinkedIn posts, general daily work. This is where you should live most of the time — it's the default for a reason.
High (Extra/xhigh)
Final polish passes on important documents (resume, cover letter, major post), or when a Standard-effort answer felt a bit thin and you want Claude to think harder.
Max
Reserve for rare, high-stakes moments — a final scholarship application, a big strategic career decision, or a genuinely hard problem where Standard/High didn't cut it. Don't use this daily; it's slower and unnecessary for routine work.

Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid

❌ Using Max effort for everyday questions — it's slower and wastes your usage for no real quality gain on simple tasks.
❌ Defaulting to Opus for routine learning/writing — Sonnet already handles this well; save Opus for genuinely hard problems.
❌ Using Haiku for creative or resume writing — it's fast but often sounds generic; not ideal for anything that represents you publicly.
❌ Never adjusting effort at all — leaving everything on default means you're either overpaying in time on simple tasks or under-thinking on important ones.
❌ Treating effort and model choice as the same lever — model = how smart, effort = how hard it thinks. You can combine Sonnet + High effort instead of jumping straight to Opus.


Final Recommendation
If you could use only ONE model and ONE effort level for most of your work:
🏆 Claude Sonnet 5 at Standard (default/High) effort
Why: It's the best all-rounder for your exact mix — sharp enough for learning support and career prep, expressive enough for creative writing, and fast enough for daily use without wasting time or tokens on simple things. Standard effort is a genuinely strong default; you'll rarely feel like you're missing quality, and you can dial up to High/Max only for the handful of moments — a final resume, a big application — that actually deserve it.
