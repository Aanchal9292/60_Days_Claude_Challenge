I built an interactive "Digital Footprint" dashboard — and it made me rethink how much a simple list of apps can reveal.

The input was just 15 everyday services: Instagram, WhatsApp, YouTube, Spotify, Amazon, Google Pay, Roblox, and a few more. No logins, no personal data, no private databases — just the names.

From that alone, the dashboard surfaces:

→ A Digital Footprint Score and Privacy Score, scored like a security audit
→ An Exposure Heatmap across social, finance, gaming, and search
→ A Company Exposure Ranking — turns out one company (Google) touched 4 of my 15 services, more than any other
→ A Risk Radar mapping identity, financial, social, and location exposure
→ A "Digital Twin" profile — the kind of inferred picture a platform could plausibly build
→ A live Privacy Improvement Simulator — uncheck an app, watch your exposure score drop in real time

The most interesting part wasn't the tech — it was seeing, clearly, how a handful of ordinary apps quietly overlap into one detailed profile. Nothing here came from a real lookup. It's just structure and inference, laid out visually instead of buried in fine print.

Sometimes the most convincing privacy lesson isn't a warning. It's a mirror.
