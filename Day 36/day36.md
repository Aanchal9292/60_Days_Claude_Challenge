Day 36/60 — Cognitive Pattern Explorer 🧠

Most self-assessment tools feel like tests. Question, click, verdict. You leave with a label and no real sense of why you think the way you do.

I wanted to see if Claude could help me build something that explores thinking patterns without ever diagnosing or boxing someone in — closer to a guided reflection than a quiz.

What I Built:
✅ A single-file, fully offline "Cognitive Pattern Explorer" — HTML, CSS, vanilla JS only
✅ Chapter 1: scenario-based questions revealing thinking tendencies (Analytical, Emotional Intuitive, Overthinking Loop, Action-First, Balanced Reflective)
✅ Chapter 2: drag-and-drop priority ranking — native HTML5 DnD + touch fallback + full keyboard support (arrow keys reorder)
✅ Chapter 3: custom draggable sliders mapping decision speed, logic vs. feeling, and overthinking tendency
✅ Final Reflection Journal: percentage breakdown across all 5 tendencies + personalized, reflective-language insights ("you often...", "this suggests...") — never absolute labels
✅ Calm/Stress ambient mode, reduced-motion support, keyboard accessibility throughout

Key Insight:
The hardest part wasn't the drag-and-drop mechanics — it was the language. Every insight had to be phrased as a tendency, not a verdict. The moment you say "You ARE an overthinker," you've turned a reflection tool into a diagnosis. Swapping to "you often revisit decisions from multiple angles" keeps the door open for the reader to agree, disagree, or just notice something new about themselves. Psychology-adjacent tools live or die by that one linguistic choice.

What's Next:
Exploring how far single-file constraints can stretch into more emotionally intelligent UX — no backend, no frameworks, just careful design.

The real skill in this challenge isn't writing prompts. It's learning to specify restraint — telling the AI exactly what NOT to claim.
