Day 27/100 — Building in Public 🚀

Today I shipped a Prior Authorization Story Simulator — an interactive, chat-based walkthrough that turns one of healthcare's most confusing processes into something anyone can actually follow.

The story: a patient (Rahul) gets diagnosed with Rheumatoid Arthritis and prescribed Humira. What happens next — the insurance review, the denial, the appeal, the approval — is exactly what real patients go through every day, told through a simple back-and-forth chat between the patient and a healthcare ops specialist.

What I learned building this:

🔹 Prior Authorization isn't just red tape. For aggressive diagnoses, delays in approval can directly affect disease progression — the AMA's 2023 PA Survey found PA causes treatment delays in the majority of cases.

🔹 Denials aren't dead ends. Most are solvable documentation gaps (like missing step therapy history) — but they still cost physician offices 2+ hours of staff time to resolve. That's a real operational burden hiding behind a single rejection letter.

🔹 Good UX can make complex systems legible. Breaking down "eligibility → clinical documentation → diagnosis match → step therapy" into plain, sequential steps made a bureaucratic black box feel understandable — even human.

🔹 Design choices carry meaning. Using an append-only chat feed, a progress bar, and branching choices wasn't just aesthetic — it mirrored how uncertainty and decision-making actually feel during a real PA journey.

Healthcare literacy shouldn't require a medical degree. If patients (and the people building healthcare products) understand why these systems exist — not just that they're frustrating — we can design better experiences around them.
